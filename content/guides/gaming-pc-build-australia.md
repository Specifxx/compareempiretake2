Building your own gaming PC is still the best value way to game on a PC in Australia, and it's easier than it looks. These parts lists are priced live: every part's price below is today's lowest in-stock price at the Australian stores we check, and the total updates every morning.

All three builds use AMD's AM5 platform, which gives you an upgrade path to newer Ryzen processors on the same motherboard.

## A note on RAM prices

Memory is unusually expensive at the moment, and it's a big share of a build's cost. Each RAM kit's page shows its price history; if prices fall, it's the part to revisit. 16GB is enough to start gaming, and it's easy to add a second kit later if your motherboard has four slots.

The Ryzen 7 9800X3D's large cache makes it one of the fastest gaming processors available. Paired with an RTX 5080, this is a high-refresh 1440p and capable 4K machine. Consider 2TB of storage at this level.

## What to add

Our lists cover the five parts we compare. You'll also need:

- **A power supply:** a quality 650W unit for builds 1 and 2, 850W for build 3. Don't buy the cheapest no-name PSU.
- **A case** with good airflow (mesh front panel) that fits your graphics card's length.
- **A CPU cooler:** the Ryzen 5 7600X and 9600X work with a good air cooler; the 9800X3D is easy to cool with a mid-range air cooler too.
- **Windows,** if you don't already have a licence.

Budget roughly $250 to $450 for these, depending on the build.

## Compatibility checklist

1. **Socket:** AM5 processors need an AM5 motherboard (B650, B850, X870).
2. **Memory:** AM5 boards take DDR5 only. DDR5-6000 is the sweet spot for Ryzen.
3. **Size:** a Micro-ATX motherboard fits most cases; check the graphics card's length against the case.
4. **BIOS:** an older board may need a BIOS update for the newest processors. Most current boards ship ready.

PCPartPicker's Australian site is useful to double-check compatibility.
