Building your own gaming PC used to be the obvious money-saver. With memory and graphics card prices up since late 2025, the gap has narrowed: Australian stores that build PCs buy parts in bulk, and their prebuilt systems come assembled, tested and with one warranty. Here is how the two compare at today's prices.

## The short answer

- **Buy prebuilt** if you want one warranty for the whole PC, don't want to assemble it, or a store's price is within about $150 of the parts.
- **Build your own** if you want to choose every part (especially the power supply and case), reuse parts you already have, or upgrade later without a store's custom parts.

## What to compare against

Our [gaming PC build guide](/guides/gaming-pc-build-australia) prices three builds part by part at the cheapest Australian store each morning. Compare a prebuilt with the build that has the same graphics card, then check what the prebuilt leaves out:

- **The power supply.** Store builds name the wattage but often not the model. A good power supply protects every other part.
- **The RAM.** 16GB is common in cheaper systems; 32GB is the comfortable amount for new games. See [the best RAM for gaming](/guides/best-ram-for-gaming-australia).
- **The SSD.** A 500GB drive fills fast with modern games. See [the best SSDs](/guides/best-ssd-australia).
- **Windows.** A prebuilt includes a licence; a build needs one on top of the parts list.

## Your rights either way

A prebuilt from an Australian store is covered by the Australian Consumer Law as one product, so a fault in any part goes back to that store. A self-build's parts are each covered separately by the store that sold them. Our [warranty guide](/guides/consumer-guarantees-warranty-electronics-australia) explains what you're entitled to.
