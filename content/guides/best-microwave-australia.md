A microwave is the appliance nobody researches and everybody owns, which is why the same model sells for $50 more at one chain than another and nobody notices. There are only three decisions: how big, how powerful, and whether you want it to do more than reheat. Prices below are read from every store we compare each morning.

## What size and power?

| Household | Capacity | Power |
|---|---|---|
| One person, reheating | 20–25L | 800–900W |
| Couple, small kitchen | 25–30L | 900–1,000W |
| Family, full dinner plates | 32–42L | 1,000–1,200W |

Capacity is the number to check: a 20-litre microwave will not take a large dinner plate flat. Power sets the speed, and anything from 1,000W cooks noticeably faster than an 800W model.

## Inverter, flatbed, convection: what the words mean

- **Inverter** microwaves (LG, Panasonic, Hisense and others) deliver steady lower power instead of switching full power on and off, so defrosting and reheating are more even. Worth the small premium.
- **Flatbed** microwaves have no turntable, so a rectangular dish fits and cleaning is a wipe. Common in Panasonic and Sharp mid-range models.
- **Convection and grill** microwaves add an oven element and a fan, so they roast and brown as well as heat. They cost more and are larger, and they replace a benchtop oven rather than a wall oven.

## Which one to buy

- **Reheating and defrosting only:** a 25-litre inverter model from the under-$200 list.
- **A family kitchen:** a 32 to 42-litre inverter model at 1,100 to 1,200W.
- **A small kitchen with no oven:** a convection microwave, and accept that it is the size of a small oven.
- **Built-in look:** some models take a trim kit to sit flush in cabinetry; it is sold separately and only fits the model it is made for.
