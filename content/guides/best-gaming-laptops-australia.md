In a gaming laptop, the graphics card decides almost everything: how many frames you get, at what resolution, and most of the price. So the best way to shop is to pick the GPU your budget allows, then find the cheapest good laptop with it.

## Which GPU do you need?

- **RTX 5050 and 3050/4050:** entry-level. Esports games and older titles at 1080p; newer games at medium settings.
- **RTX 5060:** the sweet spot for 1080p gaming at high settings, and the most common GPU in mid-range gaming laptops.
- **RTX 5070 and 5070 Ti:** for 1440p screens and high refresh rates.
- **RTX 5080 and 5090:** high-end 1440p and 4K, at a high-end price, in heavy laptops.

Laptop GPUs are not the same as desktop cards with the same name: they run at lower power, and the same GPU can perform quite differently in two laptops depending on how much power the maker allows and how well the laptop cools. Reviews that test the exact model are worth reading.

## What else matters

- **16GB of memory minimum,** 32GB for streaming or heavy multitasking. Check whether it can be upgraded.
- **The screen's refresh rate.** 144Hz or higher makes fast games look smoother, if the GPU can keep up.
- **Cooling and noise.** Thin gaming laptops run hot and loud. If you game at a desk, a thicker laptop is often faster and quieter.
- **Battery:** gaming laptops game on mains power. Unplugged, expect a few hours of light use.
