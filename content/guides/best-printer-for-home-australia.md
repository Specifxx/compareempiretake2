The cheapest printer to buy is often the most expensive to own. Many low-priced inkjets are sold close to cost because the ink cartridges are where the money is. So the right question isn't "which printer is cheapest?" but "what will it cost me per page over the next few years?"

## The three kinds of home printer

**Ink tank (EcoTank, MegaTank, Smart Tank):** refillable tanks you top up from bottles. More expensive up front, but the ink costs a fraction of cartridges per page, and a set of bottles lasts thousands of pages. Best for families and anyone printing in colour regularly.

**Laser:** uses toner. A mono (black only) laser is the cheapest way to print lots of text, and toner doesn't dry out if you print rarely. Colour lasers are good for documents but not photos.

**Cartridge inkjet:** cheap to buy, good photo quality, expensive to run. Fine if you print a few pages a month, but cartridges can dry out between uses.

## Which one should you get?

- **Mostly school and work documents, black and white:** a mono laser.
- **Homework, colour handouts and the occasional photo:** an ink tank.
- **Photos as the main job:** a photo ink tank (six colours) or a dedicated photo printer.
- **Hardly ever print:** a mono laser (toner doesn't dry out), or consider printing at a local print shop.

## Features worth having

- **Automatic double-sided printing** saves paper.
- **Wi-Fi and phone printing** (AirPrint for iPhone, Mopria for Android).
- **A scanner and copier** ("all-in-one" or "multifunction") if you scan documents. An automatic document feeder (ADF) helps for multi-page scans.
