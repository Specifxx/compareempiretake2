Prices on the same TV, laptop or coffee machine can move a lot through the year. Some of that is the big sales events; some of it is the product cycle, when a new model arrives and the old one is cleared. Here is when prices usually drop, wherever you shop, and how to check today's price against its own history.

## The sales calendar

| When | Event | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Late November | Black Friday and Cyber Monday (27 and 30 November in 2026) | Everywhere we compare |
| 11 November | Singles' Day (11.11) | Singapore and much of Asia |
| 12 December | 12.12 sales | Singapore |
| 26 December onward | Boxing Day and the post-Christmas sales | UK, Canada, Australia |
| January | January sales | UK and Australia especially |
| January–February | Lunar New Year sales | Singapore |
| June | End of financial year sales | Australia |
| July | Amazon Prime Day, and rival stores' sales the same week | US, UK, Canada, Australia, Singapore |
| Labor Day, Presidents' Day, Memorial Day | Long-weekend sales, strong for appliances | US |

## The product cycle

- **Phones:** prices on the outgoing model drop when its replacement launches (Apple in September, Samsung's flagships early in the year).
- **TVs:** new ranges arrive in spring; last year's models are cheapest from late summer to Black Friday.
- **Laptops:** new chips arrive through the year; back-to-school (July–September in the northern hemisphere) brings student deals.
- **Grills and outdoor gear:** cheapest at the end of summer.

## How to check a price is really low

Every product page on Compare Empire has a price chart going back as far as we have tracked it, and lists every store's price side by side. Before you buy, check two things: is today's price near the bottom of the chart, and is anyone else cheaper today? If both are yes, it's a good time to buy, whatever the calendar says.
