Black Friday falls on **Friday 27 November 2026**, with Cyber Monday on **30 November**. In the US it has been the biggest shopping weekend of the year for decades; in the UK, Canada, Australia and Singapore it has grown into a week or more of sales, and many stores now start in early November. The trouble is that a lot of "deals" are not deals at all: the "was" price is one the product barely sold at, or the price drops elsewhere too.

## Five checks before you buy

1. **Look at the price history, not the "was" price.** Every product page here has a price chart. If today's price was the normal price a month ago, it isn't a Black Friday deal.
2. **Compare the exact model.** Stores sometimes sell a slightly different model number for the sales (a different stand or speaker, or last year's panel). Check the model code matches.
3. **Check other stores.** A "40% off" sticker means little if three other stores sell it for the same price every week. Our product pages list every store's price side by side.
4. **Count the total.** Delivery, installation and accessories add up. A cheaper price with expensive delivery isn't cheaper.
5. **Know the returns policy.** Sale items usually have the same return and warranty rights as full-price ones, but check the store's terms before you buy something big.

## Is it worth waiting?

For TVs, headphones, consoles and laptops, prices around Black Friday are usually close to the year's lowest, and the post-Christmas sales are the other window. For brand-new launches, discounts are rare in the first months. More in [the best time to buy tech](/guides/best-time-to-buy-tech).
