For years our answer to "should I buy a foldable?" was "not yet". In 2026 it's changed to "maybe". Apple's arrival with the iPhone Duo, Samsung's three new Galaxy Z models and strong options from Google and Motorola mean foldables are now a normal choice, not an experiment. But they're still not for everyone.

## Why we've changed our minds

- **They're tough now.** Early foldables were fragile. Today's flagships have water resistance and hinges built to last years of opening and closing.
- **The creases are smaller.** You still see it in some light, but you stop noticing it within a day.
- **The outer screens are useful.** Samsung's new Fold8 and Apple's Duo both have a normal-shaped outer screen, so you don't have to open the phone for everything.
- **Apple's in.** When Apple builds iOS around a foldable, app makers follow, and the whole category gets better apps.

## Who should buy one

- **Flip phones** suit people who want a smaller phone in their pocket and a full-size screen in their hand. If you've ever wished phones were still small, try one.
- **Book foldables** suit people who read, work or watch video on the go, and who'd otherwise carry a tablet. If that's you, a Fold or the Duo can replace two devices.

## Who shouldn't

- **If the camera matters most.** Most foldables have a weaker camera than a flagship at the same price. The Galaxy Z Fold8 Ultra is the exception.
- **If you keep phones for four years or more.** A folding screen is still the most expensive part of any phone to repair.
- **If you're on a budget.** The money spent on a fold buys a lot of flagship phone instead.
