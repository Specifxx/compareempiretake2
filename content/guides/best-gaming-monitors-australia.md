Gaming monitors have become remarkably cheap in Australia. A fast 240Hz 1080p screen costs less than a game used to, and OLED, which was a luxury two years ago, is now within reach of a mid-range budget. The hard part is choosing the right resolution and refresh rate for your graphics card.

## Match the monitor to your PC

| Your graphics card | Best resolution | Refresh rate |
|---|---|---|
| Entry level (RTX 5050, RTX 5060, RX 9060 XT) | 1080p | 144–240Hz |
| Mid-range (RTX 5060 Ti, RTX 5070, RX 9070) | 1440p | 144–240Hz |
| High end (RTX 5070 Ti, RTX 5080, RX 9070 XT) | 1440p high refresh or 4K | 165–240Hz+ |
| PS5 or Xbox Series X | 4K or 1440p | 120Hz with HDMI 2.1 |

A higher refresh rate only helps if your PC can produce that many frames. For most people, **27-inch 1440p at 165Hz or more** is the sweet spot.

## The best OLED gaming monitors

OLED (and QD-OLED) monitors have near-instant pixel response, perfect blacks and stunning HDR. The caveats: they're dimmer on a bright white desktop, and static elements like taskbars can, over time, risk burn-in, which makers now address with pixel-refresh features and longer warranties. Check the warranty terms for burn-in.

## Panel types in one line each

- **IPS:** the best all-rounder: good colour and viewing angles.
- **VA:** deeper blacks, often curved, sometimes smeary in fast dark scenes.
- **TN:** fast and cheap, poor colours. Mostly gone now.
- **OLED / QD-OLED:** the best picture and motion; more expensive.
