An SSD is the cheapest way to make an old computer feel new, and the easiest PC part to over-spend on. For most people, a mid-range Gen 4 NVMe drive is all the speed they'll ever notice.

## Which kind of SSD?

- **NVMe (M.2):** the small stick that slots into a modern motherboard or laptop. The default choice for any PC from the last several years.
- **SATA (2.5-inch):** for older computers without an M.2 slot. Slower than NVMe but still far faster than a hard drive.
- **Portable (USB):** for backups and moving files. Look for USB 3.2 Gen 2 (10Gbps) or faster.

## Gen 3, Gen 4 or Gen 5?

NVMe drives come in PCIe generations. Gen 5 drives are dramatically faster on paper, but in games and everyday use the difference from a good Gen 4 drive is small, and Gen 5 drives run hotter and cost more. **For most builds, a Gen 4 drive is the value pick.** Gen 3 is fine for older systems and secondary storage.

## For a PS5

The PS5 takes an M.2 NVMe SSD in its expansion slot. Sony recommends a PCIe Gen 4 drive and a heatsink (some drives include one). Check Sony's current requirements before you buy.

## How much storage?

- **1TB** is the realistic minimum for a gaming PC today; big games take 100GB or more each.
- **2TB** is the sweet spot for price per gigabyte on many drives.
- **4TB** for video editing or a large game library.

Each drive's page shows the price for every capacity, so you can see the jump from 1TB to 2TB at each store.

## What else to check

- **DRAM cache or HMB:** drives with a DRAM cache stay fast under heavy writes; for gaming it matters less.
- **Endurance (TBW) and warranty:** five-year warranties are common on good drives.
- **Laptop fit:** most laptops take the 2280 size (22mm × 80mm); some small devices use 2230.
