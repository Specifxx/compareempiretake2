Building or upgrading a PC in Australia means choosing between a handful of specialist computer stores and the big chains. Parts are commodities: an RTX 5070 from a given brand is the same card wherever you buy it. So price, stock and service are the whole story.

We check graphics cards, CPUs, motherboards, RAM and SSDs at the major Australian PC stores every morning. Here's who is cheapest most often.

## Beyond the price

- **Stock.** A part that's cheapest at a store that's out of stock doesn't help. Our pages only count in-stock prices.
- **Delivery and pickup.** The big PC stores have showrooms in some capital cities; elsewhere, delivery cost and speed differ a lot.
- **Bundles.** Some stores bundle a game or a discount with a CPU and motherboard. Worth having, but compare the parts on their own too.
- **Warranty handling.** PC parts are usually warranted by the store first. A store close to you, or one with an easy returns process, can be worth a few dollars.
- **Build services.** If you don't want to assemble it yourself, several stores will build it for a fee.
