Under $1,000 is where laptop shopping goes wrong most often. Stores fill this price range with configurations that look fine on a shelf tag and feel slow within months: 4GB or 8GB of memory, tiny storage, dim screens. The good news is that a well-chosen laptop at this price is genuinely good for study, work and everyday use.

## The rule at this price: 16GB and an SSD

If you remember one thing: **buy 16GB of memory and at least 256GB (ideally 512GB) of SSD storage.** Windows 11 with a browser full of tabs already uses more than 8GB, and most budget laptops can't be upgraded later. A laptop with a slightly slower processor and 16GB will feel faster than a quicker chip with 8GB.

## What to avoid

- **4GB of memory.** Only acceptable on a Chromebook for a child or for very light use.
- **eMMC storage or 64GB to 128GB drives.** Windows updates alone can fill them.
- **Very old processors.** A low price can hide a chip from several generations ago. Compare the exact model in the spec.
- **TN screens and 1366×768 resolution.** Washed-out colours and poor viewing angles. Look for IPS or OLED and at least Full HD (1920×1080).

## Chromebooks

A Chromebook runs ChromeOS, which needs much less power than Windows, so a cheap one can feel snappier than a cheap Windows laptop. It suits school use (some schools ask for one), web-based work and browsing. It doesn't run regular Windows software.
