A home espresso machine pays for itself faster than most people expect, but only if you buy the right kind for how you make coffee. This guide splits machines the way buyers do (manual espresso or fully automatic) and ranks the ones most stores sell today, with the lowest in-stock price from the stores in your country.

## Manual or fully automatic?

| | Manual espresso | Fully automatic (bean to cup) |
|---|---|---|
| You do | Grind, dose, tamp, steam milk | Press a button |
| Best for | People who enjoy the process and want café-quality shots | Busy households, offices, several drinks a day |
| Also need | A good grinder, unless it has one built in | Nothing |
| Upkeep | Backflush and descale | Descale, clean the brew unit and milk system |

## The best manual espresso machines

Breville (Sage in the UK and Europe) dominates the first-machine market with the Bambino and Barista Express; Lelit, Rocket, Profitec and La Marzocco lead at the enthusiast end, with dual boilers that steam and brew at once.

## The best fully automatic machines

## How to choose

- **Budget for the grinder.** On a manual machine, the grinder matters as much as the machine. A machine with a built-in grinder is the easy start; a separate grinder is the better upgrade later. See [the best coffee grinders](/guides/best-coffee-grinders-2026).
- **Single boiler, thermoblock or dual boiler.** A thermoblock heats fast and costs less; a heat-exchanger or dual boiler lets you steam milk while pulling a shot, which matters if you make several milk drinks in a row.
- **Pressurised baskets** forgive pre-ground coffee; unpressurised ones reward fresh beans and a good grinder.
- **Check the water tank and drip tray** for size if you make coffee for more than two.
