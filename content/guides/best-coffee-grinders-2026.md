Ask any barista what to upgrade first and the answer is the grinder, not the machine. Fresh, even grounds make more difference to the cup than almost anything else. Here are the grinders the most stores sell today, with the lowest in-stock price from the stores in your country.

## The best electric grinders

Baratza's Encore and Encore ESP, Breville's (Sage's) Smart Grinder Pro, Fellow's Opus and Wilfa's grinders are the common first burr grinders; Eureka, Niche, Mazzer, Ceado and La Marzocco's Pico serve the espresso enthusiast.

## The best hand grinders

A hand grinder gives you burrs as good as an expensive electric one for much less, and it is silent. Comandante, 1Zpresso, Timemore and Hario are the names to know. Fine for one or two cups; tiring for a household.

## What to look for

- **Burrs, never blades.** Blade grinders chop unevenly, so some coffee over-extracts while the rest under-extracts. Every grinder worth buying uses burrs.
- **Espresso needs fine steps.** For espresso, look for stepless adjustment or many small steps; filter coffee is far more forgiving.
- **Single dosing or a hopper.** Single-dose grinders keep beans fresh and let you switch coffees; hoppers are quicker for one coffee every day.
- **Flat or conical burrs.** Both make excellent coffee. Flat burrs tend to give clarity in light roasts; conical burrs are common in hand grinders and forgiving for espresso.
