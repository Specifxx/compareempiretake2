## OLED, Mini LED, QLED or plain LED?

| Panel | Best at | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| OLED | Perfect blacks, wide viewing angles, film and gaming in a dim room | Lower peak brightness than the best Mini LEDs; avoid leaving static logos on for hours |
| Mini LED | Very bright HDR, bright living rooms, big sizes for the money | Some blooming around bright objects on a dark background |
| QLED | Good colour and brightness at mid-range prices | Greyer blacks than Mini LED or OLED |
| LED | The lowest price per inch | Weaker HDR, narrower viewing angles |

## What size should you buy?

Most people who upgrade wish they had gone bigger. A simple rule: for 4K, sit about 1 to 1.5 times the screen's diagonal away. At three metres (ten feet) that is a 75" to 85" TV; at two metres (six or seven feet), 55" to 65".

## What actually matters

- **Brightness and contrast** decide picture quality far more than "AI" processing names.
- **120Hz and HDMI 2.1** matter if you own a PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X or a gaming PC. Most mid-range 2026 TVs have them; budget models often don't.
- **The built-in speakers** are weak on almost every thin TV. Budget for a soundbar: see [the best soundbars of 2026](/guides/best-soundbars-2026).
- **Last year's model** is often 80–90% of the new one for much less. The price history on each TV's page shows whether today's price is genuinely low.

## When to buy

TV prices are usually at their lowest around Black Friday and in the post-Christmas sales. Before you pay, check the product's price history, not the "was" price: [how to tell a real Black Friday deal](/guides/black-friday-2026-real-tech-deals).
