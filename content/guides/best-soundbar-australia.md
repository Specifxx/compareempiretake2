Modern TVs are so thin there's no room for decent speakers. A soundbar is the single biggest upgrade you can make to a TV for the money: clearer dialogue, real bass, and with Dolby Atmos, sound that comes from above and around you.

## What the numbers mean

Soundbars are described by channels, like **5.1.2**:

- the first number is the speakers in front and around you (left, centre, right, plus rears);
- the second is the subwoofer (1 means there is one);
- the third is up-firing speakers for Dolby Atmos height effects.

A **3.1** soundbar with a subwoofer is a big step up from TV speakers. **5.1.2 or higher** with Dolby Atmos is a home-theatre experience. Separate rear speakers make the biggest difference to surround sound.

## Things to check before you buy

- **HDMI eARC.** Connect the soundbar to your TV's eARC port to get Dolby Atmos from the TV's apps and lossless formats from compatible sources. Most TVs from the last few years have it.
- **Same brand as your TV?** Samsung, LG and Sony soundbars add extras with their own TVs (using the TV speakers together with the soundbar, easier setup). Not essential, but nice.
- **Dialogue modes.** If voices are hard to hear, look for a dedicated centre channel (3.1 or higher) and a voice-enhancement mode.
- **Size.** Measure the space under your TV. A soundbar shouldn't block the screen or the TV's IR receiver.
- **Subwoofer placement.** Wireless subs need a power point but can go anywhere in the room.
