Storage is the one thing you can't upgrade on most phones, and running out is the most common reason people replace a phone that otherwise works fine. It's also the easiest way for stores to upsell you. Here's how to pick the right size.

## The short answer

- **128GB:** fine if you mainly message, browse and stream, and keep photos in the cloud.
- **256GB:** the right size for most people. Room for years of photos, apps and some downloads.
- **512GB:** for heavy 4K video, big mobile games, or keeping music and films offline.
- **1TB and up:** for people who film a lot of high-quality video on their phone.

## What uses the space

- **The system itself:** iOS or Android plus pre-installed apps take a sizeable chunk before you add anything, and it grows with updates.
- **Photos:** a few megabytes each. Thousands add up over the years.
- **Video:** the big one. 4K video at 60 frames per second uses a lot of space per minute; ProRes and similar pro formats use far more.
- **Apps and games:** most apps are small, but big games can take several gigabytes each.
- **Messaging apps:** WhatsApp and similar apps quietly fill up with photos and videos people send you.

## Check what you use now

On an **iPhone**: Settings → General → iPhone Storage. On **Android**: Settings → Storage (or Battery and device care → Storage on Samsung). Whatever you use today, buy at least double, because it only grows.

## Cloud storage: a cheaper alternative?

iCloud, Google One and similar services let you keep photos off the phone for a monthly fee. Over the years you keep a phone, that fee can add up to more than the cost of the bigger size, but it also backs up your photos if the phone is lost. Many people do both: a reasonable size, plus a cloud backup.

## Tips

- **Many current iPhones start at 256GB,** so the base model is enough for most people.
- **Samsung's bigger storage sizes are often discounted** during launch promotions.
- **Some Android phones take a microSD card,** though fewer flagships do now.
