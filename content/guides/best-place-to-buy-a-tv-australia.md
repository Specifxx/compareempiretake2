A TV is the purchase where store choice matters most. On a $2,000 TV, the gap between the cheapest and the dearest Australian store is often a few hundred dollars for exactly the same model. So which store should you check first?

## What to compare besides the price

- **Delivery and installation.** A cheap TV with a $100 delivery fee may not be cheap. Wall mounting, old-TV removal and setup are priced differently at each store.
- **The exact model code.** TV makers sell slightly different model numbers through different stores and sale events. Compare the full code, which also tells you the year.
- **Stock and pickup.** A store that has it in your local branch today can be worth a small premium for a big screen you don't want to wait for.
- **Returns.** Check the change-of-mind policy before you buy a size you're unsure about.
- **Extended warranties.** You already have strong rights under the Australian Consumer Law. Read [what you get for free](/guides/consumer-guarantees-warranty-electronics-australia) before paying for more.

## Costco and buying direct from Samsung

Costco sometimes has a TV at the lowest price, but its range is small and you need a membership. Samsung's own store tends to sit at or near RRP on TVs that retailers also stock, and in our data it's rarely the cheapest; its value is in models or bundles you can't get elsewhere.

## Don't forget to haggle

TVs are one of the products where asking still works, especially at Harvey Norman (whose stores are franchised) and on bigger screens. Take the cheapest price from this page and [ask them to match it](/guides/how-to-price-match-australia).
