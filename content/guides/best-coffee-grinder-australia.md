Every barista will tell you the same thing: the grinder matters more than the machine. A fresh, even grind is what makes espresso sweet and filter coffee clean, and a $300 grinder on a $500 machine beats the reverse every time. The catch in Australia is that the same grinder is often $40 to $100 apart between stores on the same day, because coffee gear is sold by specialist roasters and online shops as much as by the big chains. Prices below are read from every store we compare each morning.

## Burr or blade?

A **blade grinder** chops beans unevenly, so some grounds over-extract (bitter) while others under-extract (sour). A **burr grinder** crushes them between two surfaces to a set size. Every grinder in this guide is a burr grinder; a blade grinder is only worth it if you buy ground coffee anyway and want to grind spices.

Within burr grinders, the question is **what you brew**:

- **Espresso** needs a fine, adjustable grind and the ability to change it in tiny steps. This is the expensive end.
- **Filter, pour-over and AeroPress** need a medium grind and even particle size; most mid-range electric grinders and good hand grinders do this well.
- **French press and cold brew** need a coarse grind; almost any burr grinder will do.

## Which one to buy

- **Filter coffee, one or two cups a day:** a hand grinder from the list above. Spend the saving on better beans.
- **Filter coffee for a household:** an electric grinder under $300 with a stepped adjustment.
- **Espresso on a home machine:** budget at least as much for the grinder as a third of the machine's price, and buy one with stepless or very fine-stepped adjustment.
- **Both espresso and filter from one grinder:** possible, but changing between the two settings every day is tedious; many people end up with a hand grinder for filter and an electric grinder dialled in for espresso.

## Before you buy

- **Check the burr type.** Conical burrs are the norm; flat burrs give a cleaner, brighter cup for espresso and cost more. The product's page lists what the store states.
- **Check retention.** Single-dose grinders hold almost no grounds between uses; hopper grinders keep a few grams, which go stale. If you change beans often, single-dose is worth the premium.
- **Add shipping.** Specialist stores often ship free over a threshold and the chains offer click and collect; our prices are item prices.
