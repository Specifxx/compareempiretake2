Carriers make a phone on a plan look affordable: "$55 a month" sounds a lot easier than "$1,999". But the monthly figure hides two things: how much you're paying for the phone itself, and how long you're locked in for. So we lined them up.

Every morning we read the device repayments Telstra, Optus and Vodafone list for each phone, and compare the total with the cheapest outright price at any Australian store.

## Outright vs plan, phone by phone

[[plans:n=12]]

Read the table carefully, because the answer flips from phone to phone. On most phones, buying outright from the cheapest store costs the same or less than the carrier's repayments. But carriers regularly run bonus credits and "save $X" offers on particular models, and when they do, the phone on a plan can genuinely come out cheaper. Those rows show in green.

## The plan column is only half the cost

Device repayments are on top of the plan itself. A phone that's "$47 a month over 36 months" on a $65 plan costs you $112 a month, and you're committed for three years. If you leave early you'll usually have to pay out the rest of the phone.

So compare like with like:

- **Outright:** the phone price, plus whatever SIM-only plan you choose (including cheap prepaid and smaller providers that run on the big networks).
- **On a plan:** the device repayments, plus that carrier's plan for the whole term.

For many people, the outright phone plus a cheaper SIM-only plan is the bigger saving, even when the device repayments look similar.

## When a plan makes sense

- **The carrier is running a real discount** on the phone you want (look for a green row above).
- **You want 0% finance.** Spreading the cost with no interest is a fair reason, if you were going to stay with that carrier anyway.
- **You'd stay on that carrier's plan regardless.** Then the lock-in costs you nothing extra.

## When outright wins

- **You want a cheap SIM-only plan.** Smaller providers are often much cheaper per month than the big three's own plans.
- **You might switch.** Outright means no payout if you change networks.
- **You want to resell or hand down the phone.** It's yours from day one, unlocked.
- **A store has it on sale.** Retail discounts on outright prices are common, especially on last year's models. Check [today's phone deals](/deals/phones).
