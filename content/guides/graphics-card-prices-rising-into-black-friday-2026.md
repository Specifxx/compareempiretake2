Every November, prices on almost everything we track drift down as stores clear stock before Black Friday. This year one category is doing the opposite. We have been recording the cheapest Australian price of every product each morning since 23 October, and in those twelve days graphics cards moved up, hard, while the rest of the site moved the way it should. This is what the numbers show and our read on why.

## What moved, 23 October to 4 November

Comparing each product's cheapest in-stock price on the first morning with the last:

- **Graphics cards:** 171 of 310 cards got dearer by more than 3%. Nine got cheaper. By model, the typical RTX 5080 rose 25%, the RTX 5090 18%, the RX 9070 XT 14%, the RTX 5050 11% and the RTX 5070 8%. Even the Intel Arc B580, the budget card, rose 14% at every store that stocks it.
- **TVs:** of the TVs sold by three or more stores, 17 got cheaper and 2 dearer.
- **Laptops:** 13 cheaper, 5 dearer. **Tablets:** 9 cheaper, none dearer. **Phones:** 9 cheaper, 7 dearer, and the flagships barely moved at all: the iPhone 17 Pro Max's cheapest price went from $1,999 to $1,879, and the Galaxy S26 Ultra, Fold8 and iPhone Duo did not change.

That is the normal pre-sale pattern everywhere except the one category where the makers, not the stores, set the direction.

## Why graphics cards are rising

It is not the stores. The same cards rose at every retailer we compare, by similar amounts, in the same week, which is what a cost increase upstream looks like. The cause is memory. The AI data-centre build-out has taken so much of the world's DRAM capacity that the GDDR6 and GDDR7 chips on a graphics card have become the expensive part. AMD told its board partners it would raise card prices by at least 10% for 2026; Nvidia raised prices on parts of the GeForce range in January and May, and in August was reported to be weighing a further 20 to 30% rise as GDDR7 and TSMC wafer costs climbed, with RTX 50 cards in South Korea rising as much as 30% that month. What we are seeing in Australian stores in late October looks like that increase reaching the shelf, after existing stock sold through at the old prices.

The pattern inside the data fits that: the cards with the most memory rose most. The RTX 5080 (16 GB of GDDR7) and 5090 (32 GB) led; the 8 GB RTX 5060 Ti barely moved. If you are choosing between two cards with different memory sizes, the gap between them is wider now than it was a month ago, and we think it stays that way through the sales.

## What that means for Black Friday

Our honest take: do not wait for a Black Friday graphics card deal. The stores cannot discount what they bought at a higher price, and the historical Black Friday GPU bargains came in years when the makers were clearing stock, not rationing it. If you need a card, the cheapest store today is as good as it is likely to get until next year, and the spread between stores on the same card is often $100 or more, which is a bigger saving than any sale will bring.

## Where we think it goes

Memory prices are the thing to watch, not graphics cards. Industry forecasts have DRAM supply short through 2026, and a PC's RAM and SSD use the same wafers as a graphics card's memory, so we expect RAM kits and SSDs to follow cards up over the next few months. Our [RAM guide](/guides/best-ram-for-gaming-australia) and [SSD guide](/guides/best-ssd-australia) show today's prices; if those start climbing the way cards did, that is the second wave arriving. The next release that could change the picture is a new generation of cards with less memory per dollar, and nothing like that is due before 2027.
